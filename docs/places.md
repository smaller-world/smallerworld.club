# Places

AI-generated doc on places in smaller world.

## The dock

I’d make the dock somewhere you can go **before you know what you need**. The
workshop is where you go when you want to work on something. The dock is where
you go when you’ve had a day and aren’t sure what to make of it yet. The little
person fishing in your sketch already makes sitting there feel like a valid
thing to do.

### A visit to the dock

On the home map, the dock’s lantern comes on around evening. Tapping it takes
you to the page you drew. Right under _welcome to the dock_, I’d try three small
invitations:

- **write a little** opens the editor you already have.
- **talk for a while** lets you speak in fragments, pause, and keep the
  recording alongside the transcript.
- **just sit** gives the illustration the whole screen. The bobber moves. Maybe
  a duck passes. You can leave whenever you like.

I’d make the daily recommendation the **lantern on the map**. It gives the world
an evening rhythm and lets you come on your own time. The dock is still open at
9 a.m., and if you’ve been away for a week, the lantern is simply on again when
you get back.

## What the dock helps you do

The part I’d most want to get right is **coming back to something you’ve been
carrying**. Say you’ve talked about feeling far away from a friend a few times.
On a later visit, you could choose to open that thread and see three short
excerpts from different days, with the original entries a tap away. Maybe you
realize what changed. Maybe you just want somewhere to put today’s version of
it.

That suggests a few things a reflection can become:

- **An entry.** Save the words, voice note, or photo of a notebook page as you
  left it. Most visits can end here.
- **A thread you’re keeping an eye on.** Give it a name in your own words, like
  _why sundays feel weird_. On another visit, you can add to it or ask _what’s
  changed since last time?_
- **Something to take elsewhere.** Highlight _i want to draw the houses on my
  street_ and send that line to the garden. If it becomes _take my sketchbook on
  saturday_, send it to the workshop. Both can point back to the entry where the
  thought started.

That’s where I see the “Workflowy on autopilot” idea getting interesting. You
can ramble for five minutes and the app can offer to pull out one or two things
worth keeping track of. You decide what gets a place. I’d also give paper and
voice the same standing as typed entries. A photo of a messy notebook page
should feel like a finished entry, with searchable text available when you need
it.

### Fish, and other things the water does

My favorite version is **one optional cast after your first visit of the day**,
even if you only came to sit. You can save a reflection and close the app, or
stick around to see what’s biting. I’d let the weather or time of day influence
what appears and let _you_ decide whether the fish means anything.

A few catches I’d want to find:

- **the fish of sadness** — _it likes to be held for a moment before you let it
  go._
- **the fish of almost calling someone** — _it keeps swimming close to shore._
- **the fish of i actually had a nice day** — _common, but surprisingly hard to
  spot._
- **the fish of the thing you remembered in the shower** — _slippery._
- **somebody’s old key** — weeks later, you spot a tiny locked drawer under the
  dock bench.

You could **keep a sketch** of the fish in a field notebook or **let it go** and
watch it swim away. I’d leave the notebook unnumbered. Finding a funny fish on a
hard day feels different when there’s no empty slot telling you what to catch
next.

I’d give the dock a few slower changes, too. The waterline rises after rain. A
spider builds a web between two posts over several visits. The mail carrier
sometimes stops at the end of the dock to eat lunch. Those little events give
the place a life of its own, and they make returning interesting even on days
when you have nothing to write.

### How it fits into the world

I like the idea that places have **different manners**. The dock is quiet and
asks very little. The garden lets unfinished ideas sit around. The workshop
helps you make a decision and find the next step. The gallery lets you look at
things you’ve made without immediately reopening them for editing. A single
thought can travel through all four while your original words stay where you put
them.

That gives _Begin Again_ and the mailbox distinct jobs, too. The morning bird
might bring you **one thing you asked to carry forward** from yesterday. The
mail carrier can deliver something less urgent, like a photo you asked to see
again in a year. The raccoon could be someone you go find when you want a second
perspective. I’d let the dock itself stay mostly quiet.

For the first pass, I’d try **a private reflection you can write or speak, a
lantern on the home map, and a single optional cast**. The thing I’d pay
attention to is whether you still want to visit after you’ve seen the same fish
twice. I’d save the larger collection and the other places until you’ve had a
chance to live with the dock for a few ordinary days.
